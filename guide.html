A starter guide to Bangladeshi civil documents

Help desk terms: this guide is free and is not legal advice. We never ask for ID numbers, scans of IDs or payments. Please do not email them to us.

Many people come to old records because of a present-day paperwork problem: a missing birth certificate, an NID that doesn't match a passport, or a parent's land that needs to pass to heirs. These are the official starting points.

This guide is general information, not legal advice. Fees, forms and rules change. Always confirm the current process with the official site or with your nearest Bangladesh embassy or consulate before you pay or travel.

1 · Birth registration

Get or correct a birth certificate

  1. Apply online through the government's Birth and Death Registration Information System (BDRIS), which issues a 17-digit registration number.
  2. If you were born abroad, submit the printed application through the Bangladesh embassy or consulate for your area.
  3. Corrections are usually made by the office that first issued the certificate. Bring proof of your name in English and Bangla, and proof of permanent address.

Official: bdris.gov.bd

2 · National ID (NID)

Apply for an NID from abroad

  1. You need a valid Bangladeshi passport and an online-verifiable 17-digit birth registration. The details on both must match exactly.
  2. Fill in the application on the Election Commission's NID portal, then book biometric enrolment at your embassy or consulate, if it offers the service.
  3. Get the birth record right first. Most NID problems start with a spelling or date mismatch there.

Official: services.nidw.gov.bd

3 · Land and inheritance

Find a family land record

  1. Land is recorded by mouza (revenue village) and khatian (record of rights). Survey series you will meet include CS, SA, RS and BS.
  2. Certified khatian copies and mouza maps can be ordered online from the land ministry's e-Porcha portal.
  3. Inheritance usually needs a succession certificate or warishan certificate from the local union parishad or municipality, then a mutation (namjari) at the land office.

Official: eporcha.gov.bd · land.gov.bd

Where the old books help

If a family document names a thana, pargana or mouza you can't place, look it up in the district gazetteer for that area in the catalog above. Gazetteers list subdivisions and police stations as they were around 1910, which often explains a place name on an old deed.

In the United States, consular services are offered by the Embassy of Bangladesh in Washington, D.C. and the Consulate General in New York.

Glossary

Terms you will meet in old land and legal records

Spellings vary in English sources. Ask the local land office if a term's meaning in your family's district is unclear.

Bigha, kani
Traditional land-area units. Their size varied by region, so a record in bigha needs a local conversion.
Dag
A numbered plot on a survey map. Land records cite the dag number.
Dolil
A deed, such as a sale or gift of land.
Jote, raiyat
A cultivating tenant's holding, and the tenant who held it.
Khatian
A record of rights: who owns or holds which plots in a village. Several survey series exist (CS, SA, RS).
Mouza
The basic revenue village, with its own survey map and records.
Namjari (mutation)
Updating the land record after a sale or inheritance so the new owner's name appears.
Porcha
A certified copy of a khatian. Land offices issue it, and the eporcha.gov.bd portal serves it online.
Taluk
A land holding or sub-estate, often below a zamindar.
Thana, upazila
The police-station area, now the upazila, used to place a village within a district.
Zamindar
A landlord who collected revenue for the government under the Permanent Settlement of 1793.
Subah, Diwani
A Mughal province, and the right to collect its revenue, which the East India Company received in 1765.
