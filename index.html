About

Why an archive, and why so small

Families from Bengal often know their desh, the home village, by name only. The records that explain it, such as the colonial district gazetteers, the 1901 and 1911 censuses and the land surveys, are scattered across digital libraries and hard to find if you don't know their titles. This site gathers the useful ones in one place, with a short note on why each matters.

The archive has two jobs. The first is a reading room: a catalog of pre-1931 books on Bengal, with the full text of some of them hosted here. The second is a help desk guide that points people to the right official office for a birth certificate, an NID or a land record, and uses the old sources to explain the places and terms they find on their family's papers.

Colonial-era sources are primary evidence, not neutral truth. Where a book's framing of caste, religion or race is dated, its card says so.

How it started

The archive began in 2026 as a family research project. A printed lawyer's diary from 1927, a pocket calendar and a letter from 1913, and a shelf of books about Bengal raised more questions than the family could answer: which district a village belonged to, what an unfamiliar legal term meant, where to find the matching official record today.

Answering those questions meant reading the same gazetteers and censuses over and over. The founder, who lives in Maryland, decided to catalog them once, with notes, so the next family does not start from zero. Founder's note: add two or three personal sentences and a name here.

What we do and don't do

We do

  • Catalog and host public-domain books about Bengal, with notes on their context.
  • Explain which official office handles which Bangladeshi civil document.
  • Scan and return family papers, and publish only what the family approves.

We don't

  • Give legal advice, or act as an agent or lawyer for anyone.
  • Handle fees, original documents, ID numbers or payments.
  • Accept donations, sell anything, or track visitors.

Diaspora help desk

A starter guide to Bangladeshi civil documents

Help desk terms: this guide is free and is not legal advice. We never ask for ID numbers, scans of IDs or payments. Please do not email them to us.

Many people come to old records because of a present-day paperwork problem: a missing birth certificate, an NID that doesn't match a passport, or a parent's land that needs to pass to heirs. These are the official starting points.

This guide is general information, not legal advice. Fees, forms and rules change. Always confirm the current process with the official site or with your nearest Bangladesh embassy or consulate before you pay or travel.

1 · Birth registration

Get or correct a birth certificate

  1. Apply online through the government's Birth and Death Registration Information System (BDRIS), which issues a 17-digit registration number.
  2. If you were born abroad, submit the printed application through the Bangladesh embassy or consulate for your area.
  3. Corrections are usually made by the office that first issued the certificate. Bring proof of your name in English and Bangla, and proof of permanent address.

Official: bdris.gov.bd

2 · National ID (NID)

Apply for an NID from abroad

  1. You need a valid Bangladeshi passport and an online-verifiable 17-digit birth registration. The details on both must match exactly.
  2. Fill in the application on the Election Commission's NID portal, then book biometric enrolment at your embassy or consulate, if it offers the service.
  3. Get the birth record right first. Most NID problems start with a spelling or date mismatch there.

Official: services.nidw.gov.bd

3 · Land and inheritance

Find a family land record

  1. Land is recorded by mouza (revenue village) and khatian (record of rights). Survey series you will meet include CS, SA, RS and BS.
  2. Certified khatian copies and mouza maps can be ordered online from the land ministry's e-Porcha portal.
  3. Inheritance usually needs a succession certificate or warishan certificate from the local union parishad or municipality, then a mutation (namjari) at the land office.

Official: eporcha.gov.bd · land.gov.bd

Where the old books help

If a family document names a thana, pargana or mouza you can't place, look it up in the district gazetteer for that area in the catalog above. Gazetteers list subdivisions and police stations as they were around 1910, which often explains a place name on an old deed.

In the United States, consular services are offered by the Embassy of Bangladesh in Washington, D.C. and the Consulate General in New York.

Context

Bengal in ten dates

A short frame for reading the catalog. The gazetteers, for instance, were written for the province of Eastern Bengal and Assam, which existed only from 1905 to 1912.

    Standards

    How we decide what goes on this site

    Rights

    • Only works first published in 1930 or earlier, which are in the public domain in the United States. Authors of the listed books also died long enough ago to be out of copyright in India and Bangladesh.
    • Later reprints and new editions are not used, because they can carry new copyright in introductions or typesetting.
    • Modern books and articles are never posted without written permission from the author or heirs.

    People and privacy

    • Family papers and photographs are shared only with the family's written consent.
    • We leave out the names, addresses and faces of living people unless they agree in writing.
    • Anyone can ask us to remove an item, and we take it down.

    Accuracy and context

    • Every card names its source and the rights basis. We say when we have not verified a scan.
    • Colonial sources get a context note. We present them as evidence of what officials believed, not as settled fact.
    • Corrections are welcome. Write to us with the call number, and we fix the card and note the date.

    How to cite this archive

    Cite the original work first, then the archive's call number and the address of the page you used. For example:

    Allen, B. C. Dacca. Eastern Bengal and Assam District Gazetteers, 1912. Waymark Project, Bengal Heritage Archive, call no. BHA·GAZ·1912·01. Scan: archive.org/details/daccaallen00alle. Accessed date.

    To cite the site itself: Waymark Project. Bengal Heritage Archive. Accessed date. site address.

    Call numbers are made of the theme (GAZ, HIS, MAP, SOC, LIT, READ or FAM), the year of publication, and a running number.

    Glossary

    Terms you will meet in old land and legal records

    Spellings vary in English sources. Ask the local land office if a term's meaning in your family's district is unclear.

    Bigha, kani
    Traditional land-area units. Their size varied by region, so a record in bigha needs a local conversion.
    Dag
    A numbered plot on a survey map. Land records cite the dag number.
    Dolil
    A deed, such as a sale or gift of land.
    Jote, raiyat
    A cultivating tenant's holding, and the tenant who held it.
    Khatian
    A record of rights: who owns or holds which plots in a village. Several survey series exist (CS, SA, RS).
    Mouza
    The basic revenue village, with its own survey map and records.
    Namjari (mutation)
    Updating the land record after a sale or inheritance so the new owner's name appears.
    Porcha
    A certified copy of a khatian. Land offices issue it, and the eporcha.gov.bd portal serves it online.
    Taluk
    A land holding or sub-estate, often below a zamindar.
    Thana, upazila
    The police-station area, now the upazila, used to place a village within a district.
    Zamindar
    A landlord who collected revenue for the government under the Permanent Settlement of 1793.
    Subah, Diwani
    A Mughal province, and the right to collect its revenue, which the East India Company received in 1765.

    Questions

    Frequently asked

    Can I download and share these books?

    Yes. Every book in the catalog was published in 1930 or earlier and is in the public domain in the United States. The authors died long enough ago that the works are also out of copyright in Bangladesh and India. Some scans carry a library watermark, so credit the library that scanned them.

    Which books are hosted here, and which are links?

    Every Internet Archive book in the catalog has a copy hosted here as PDF files, and the five Reading room works are hosted too. Large books are split into parts of about 25 MB or less. The five Project Gutenberg entries are ebooks, so they link to Gutenberg. Titles always link to the original scan, which is the full-quality copy.

    Why are some family papers closed?

    Unpublished letters and diaries belong to the writer's heirs, and they can mention people who are still alive. We list them so researchers know they exist, and open them only with the family's approval.

    Can you fix my birth certificate or NID for me?

    No. We are volunteers, not lawyers or agents, and we never handle fees or original documents. We can point you to the right office and explain the terms on an old record.

    Is this a registered charity?

    Not yet. Waymark Project is an independent, volunteer-run project. We don't accept donations, and nothing given to us is tax-deductible.

    I found a mistake in a catalog card.

    Thank you. Write to us with the call number on the card and we will correct it.
